# Curriculum Blueprint — 114-Hour Target

**Status:** Proposed structure for curriculum review; not approved instructional content.
**Audience:** Product, chess curriculum authors, learning designers, engineering.
**Learners:** Ages 5–13; age bands 5–7, 8–10, 11–13.

## Purpose and constraints

This blueprint allocates a 114-hour target across learning domains so the team can validate scope. Hours are planned active learning time, not a promise of time-to-mastery. Do not publish these allocations as final until a qualified chess educator reviews lesson content, prerequisites, age adaptations, and assessment thresholds.

## Proposed hour allocation

| Domain | Hours | Outcomes |
|---|---:|---|
| Orientation, board coordinates, and chess goals | 6 | Identify board/squares, setup, objective, and basic game flow |
| Piece movement and captures | 12 | Identify legal movement and capture patterns |
| Check, checkmate, stalemate, and king safety | 10 | Recognize check and distinguish terminal states |
| Special moves and draw rules | 8 | Apply castling, en passant, promotion, and relevant draw rules |
| Opening principles | 10 | Develop pieces, contest center, safeguard king, avoid common traps |
| Tactical patterns | 18 | Recognize forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks, double attacks, and basic mating patterns |
| Calculation and candidate-move habits | 12 | Identify threats, consider forcing moves, compare candidate moves |
| Basic endgames | 12 | Apply king/pawn fundamentals and selected rook/minor-piece principles |
| Strategy and positional play | 10 | Use material, activity, king safety, pawn structure, and plans at an age-appropriate level |
| Game review and decision reflection | 8 | Review turning points and explain decisions without shame or blame |
| Integrated practice and mastery checks | 8 | Demonstrate transfer across mixed positions and games |
| **Total** | **114** | |

## Suggested progression

1. **Foundations:** board, pieces, movement, captures, check, checkmate.
2. **Rules fluency:** special moves, draws, legal move discipline.
3. **Tactics:** threats, basic patterns, forcing moves.
4. **Calculation:** candidate moves, short variations, blunder checks.
5. **Endgames:** king activity, pawn promotion, selected basic endings.
6. **Strategy:** development, plans, pawn structure, piece activity.
7. **Integration:** annotated games, mixed puzzles, reflective review.

This sequence is a proposal. The curriculum team may rearrange topics where prerequisites and assessment evidence support it.

## Age-band adaptations

- **5–7:** short sessions, concrete examples, large touch targets, limited text, visual demonstrations, repetition, audio only as an optional aid.
- **8–10:** short explanations, guided discovery, simple notation introduced gradually, explicit “why” feedback.
- **11–13:** deeper calculation, notation, candidate-move comparison, more independent analysis.

Keep learning objectives and rules consistent; vary language, pacing, scaffolding, and task complexity. Do not infer chess ability from age alone.

## Lesson specification

Every lesson record should include:
- Stable ID, title, version, domain, age-band variants, estimated active minutes.
- Learning objectives and prerequisite IDs.
- Instructional content and worked example.
- Guided practice and independent practice.
- Assessment item IDs, accepted answer/position, difficulty, and explanation.
- Mastery rule/version, retry limits if any, remediation route, and review recommendation.
- Accessibility alternatives, localization status, reviewer, approval state, and content source/license.

## Mastery policy proposal

Mastery must be based on assessment evidence across more than one item or attempt. A completion click, time spent, streak, or single lucky guess is insufficient. The curriculum team must define thresholds, evidence window, spacing/review behavior, and reset/versioning policy before production use.

Record at minimum: objective/skill ID, item version, attempt result, timestamp, hint usage where appropriate, and mastery algorithm version. Avoid retaining full free-text or audio when a compact event is sufficient.

## Content quality gates

Before a lesson is released:
1. Chess expert validates every position, legal move, solution, and explanation.
2. Learning designer validates objective alignment, progression, and age appropriateness.
3. Accessibility review validates text, visuals, interaction, and non-audio alternatives.
4. Engineering validates schema, rendering, engine compatibility, and automated puzzle checks.
5. Product/privacy review validates data collection and any external content/license.
6. Content receives an immutable version and explicit approval status.

## Open decisions

- Approve or revise the proposed hour allocation.
- Define the exact lesson inventory and module count.
- Define numeric mastery thresholds and review intervals.
- Decide localization and notation conventions for launch.
- Name qualified reviewers and content licensing process.
